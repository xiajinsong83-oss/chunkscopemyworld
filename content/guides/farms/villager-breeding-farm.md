Villagers are the best renewable resource in the game — a breeding pair turns into a self-sustaining population that supplies enchanted books, tools and emeralds. Breeding only needs **beds, food and a working villager**.

## What villagers need to breed

- **Beds**: each baby needs a bed with 2 blocks of headroom. More beds than villagers = they keep breeding.
- **Food**: bread (3), carrots/potatoes/beetroot (12) per willing villager. Throw it at them or use a farmer's auto-harvest.
- **Willingness**: a villager becomes "willing" after collecting 12 food and sharing it — that's what lets them breed.

## A simple 2-tier breeder

1. Put the breeding pair in a small chamber with a **farmer** who farms a carrot/potato patch.
2. Add **10–20 beds** below, with a water drop so babies fall out of reach of the parents.
3. Babies grow in ~20 minutes (feed them food to speed it up — it halves their growth).

## The trading hall

- Give each villager a **job block** (lectern, smithing table, cartographer table…) to assign a profession.
- Lock trades by **trading once** — the villager keeps its offers forever, and future trades refresh stock, not the offer.
- **Librarians (lectern)**: cycle the lectern until the trade you want (e.g. Mending) appears, then lock it.
- Group workstations 2 blocks apart and light everything; **nitwits** (green coat, no job) can be discarded.

## Emerald economy tips

- Farmers are the fastest emerald source: trade wheat/melons/pumpkins in bulk.
- Fletchers turn sticks into emeralds cheaply early game.
- Use the **Farm Calculator** on ChunkScope to size your crop fields to the villager demand.

## Common mistakes

- **No extra beds** — villagers stop breeding when beds run out.
- **Not enough headroom** over beds — babies suffocate, population stalls.
- **Zombie siege** — light your village and wall it; golems help.
