Honey bottles are a top-tier food (6 hunger) and honeycomb makes **honeycomb blocks** (used in waxed copper and candles). A single hive + a few flowers is all you need for a steady supply.

## Getting bees

- Find bee **nests** in flower forests and plains (white flowers help).
- **Breed them** with flowers: two bees fed a flower produce a baby.
- Move bees by breaking a nest with **Silk Touch** and placing it where you want — or breed near a planted flower patch.

## The simple manual farm

1. Build an **apiary**: place 3–6 hives in a row, surrounded by flowers.
2. When a hive reaches **honey level 5** (pollen visible dripping), collect:
   - **Honey bottles**: use a glass bottle (empty) on the hive → honey bottle. Bee's don't anger when you don't harvest the honeycomb.
   - **Honeycomb**: use shears → 3 honeycomb. Bees get angry (smoke the hive first with a campfire underneath to calm them).
3. Campfire under each hive lets you harvest **without angering** the bees.

## Fully automatic harvest

- Put a **dispenser** with shears above each hive, a hopper collecting below into a chest.
- An **observer** on the hive detects when it's full and fires the dispenser → shears → honeycomb drops into the hopper.
- Add a glass-bottle auto-harvest version for honey by piping empty bottles into the dispenser.

## What to do with the output

- **Honey bottles** restore 6 hunger and cure poison — great emergency food and a potion ingredient.
- **Honeycomb** → honeycomb blocks → **waxed copper** (stops oxidation) and **candles** (decorative light).
- Crafting honeycomb with candles gives the classic honeycomb block; bees still love flowers nearby for new hives.

## Quick tips

- **Smoke the hive** (campfire underneath) before harvesting honeycomb — otherwise bees attack.
- **Shears in a dispenser** work automatically; the **observer + dispenser** pattern is the same one used in many redstone farms.
- Keep **at least 2 hives** so breeding doesn't stall; bees only work during the day and return pollen to the hive.
